# Impeccable Design Context — Cartra AI

## Design Context

### Users
**Primary audience**: Business owners, CEOs, and operations managers at mid-market companies (50-500 employees) in logistics, legal, finance, insurance, and manufacturing.

**Context when arriving**: They've heard about AI agents but don't know where to start, or they've been burned by vague AI promises. They're evaluating whether Cartra is the team to trust with a real implementation. They need to feel confident fast — these are decision-makers who scan, don't read.

**Job to be done**: Determine in under 60 seconds whether Cartra has the expertise, credibility, and process to deliver measurable results with custom AI agents. Then book a discovery call.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: Thoughtful, intelligent, sharp.

**If Cartra were a person**: A senior engineer who became a consultant — speaks plainly, backs claims with data, doesn't oversell, but radiates quiet confidence. The kind of person who draws a diagram on a whiteboard and suddenly everything clicks.

**Emotional goals**: When a prospect lands on the site, they should feel:
- "This team is sharp and moves fast"
- "These people are ahead of the curve"
- "This is a safe, reliable partner with deep expertise"

### Aesthetic Direction

**Reference**: [Ramp](https://ramp.com) — clean, modern, polished. Powerful and punchy copy. Data-driven and rich with animation, but doesn't feel generic or too SaaS-y.

**Specific observations from Ramp (for direct visual reference)**:
- **Light mode with a single electric accent**: Warm off-white backgrounds, near-black text, with chartreuse/lime yellow (≈#EBF123) reserved exclusively for the primary CTA ("See a demo" / "Become a partner"). The accent works because it's rare.
- **Varied card backgrounds, not uniform white**: Bento-style sections use *different* subtle warm tints for each card — beige, cream, soft gray, pale blue — so the grid has variety without needing borders or shadows. Cards read as distinct without shouting.
- **Large, confident display type**: Headlines are set tight, heavy, and big. Near-black. Centered or left-aligned depending on the section.
- **Mixed-color headlines for emphasis**: Two-part headlines use black for the key phrase and medium gray for the secondary clause (e.g., "AI that learns from your team. Powered by 50,000 others that came before you."). Creates rhythm and hierarchy within a single sentence.
- **Punchy parallel construction**: "Scale the team. Shrink the paperwork." / "Built for finance. Loved by every client." Short declarative sentences, often paired.
- **Product UI as the hero visual**: Real interface mockups — approval flows, spend tables, dashboards — placed inside the bento cards, not abstract illustrations or stock photography. Occasional tastefully-staged team photos with callout annotations on them (e.g., "There's a 14% increase in bill pay volume").
- **Soft rounded corners, no hard borders**: Cards use medium radius, subtle tinted backgrounds, and the faintest shadow. They feel like panels, not boxed-off sections.
- **Dotted/halftone textures**: Patterns like the world map rendered as dots — technical but warm.

**Translation to Cartra**: Borrow Ramp's typographic discipline (heavy grotesque display, mixed-color two-part headlines, punchy parallel construction, product UI as hero visual, bento layouts with tinted-card variety) — but drop Ramp's single-accent discipline in favor of Retell's richer navy + cobalt + peach color system. Cartra should feel confident like Ramp, thoughtful like Cursor, and energetic like Retell.

**Theme**: Light-dominant with bold dark and warm-color panel accents. Cool-neutral off-white base (Retell-inspired) rather than warm cream. Deep midnight navy appears as dramatic section panels for emphasis, not as a full dark-mode treatment.

**Second reference**: [Cursor](https://cursor.com)

**Specific observations from Cursor (direct visual + CSS inspection)**:
- **Exact palette**: Background `#f7f7f4` (warm off-white), card/chrome `#f2f1ed` (slightly darker warm beige), text `#26251e` (warm near-black). All borders and muted surfaces are `color-mix(in oklab, #26251e X%, transparent)` — a single-hue color system where everything derives from the text color at different opacities.
- **Custom display typeface**: "CursorGothic" — a humanist grotesque sans at weight 400 (not bold), tight negative letter-spacing. Confidence through restraint, not heft.
- **Serif body**: Body paragraphs set in "jjannon" (old-style serif, Iowan Old Style fallback) at 55% opacity of the text color. A bold editorial move that makes Cursor feel like a literary/craft brand rather than a SaaS.
- **Italic emphasis inside headlines**: "Built to make you *extraordinarily* productive..." — italicized words create rhythm and personality within otherwise restrained type.
- **Orange accent** (≈#f15a24): Reserved for link text with ↗ arrow ("Learn about agentic development →", "Explore models ↗"). Used more freely than Ramp's chartreuse but still specifically for "go deeper" links, not every button.
- **Pill-shaped CTAs**: Primary "Download for macOS ↓" in warm near-black, secondary "Request a demo →" as transparent ghost — both extremely rounded.
- **Painterly backgrounds behind product UI**: Hero shows layered macOS windows floating over an impressionist-style painting (looks Monet-esque). Distinctive move — art as backdrop for product screenshots. Completely uncommon in SaaS.
- **Logos in individual pill cards**: Instead of a plain logo row, each customer logo (Stripe, OpenAI, Linear, Datadog, NVIDIA, Figma, Ramp, Adobe) sits in its own rounded rectangle with subtle warm gray background. Tasteful alternative to the standard logo cloud.
- **Testimonials in plain quoted prose**: 3x2 grid of testimonial cards on the warm-gray surface. No fancy blockquote design — just the quote in quotation marks, then avatar + name + title at bottom. Restrained trust-building.
- **Product UI deeply embedded in feature cards**: The "Stay on the frontier" bento has three cards, each with real product screenshots showing actual functionality (model picker dropdown, codebase search, code editor). UI *is* the illustration.
- **Complex product screenshots with visible code**: Signals technical depth without explaining.

**Third reference**: [Retell AI](https://retellai.com)

**Specific observations from Retell (direct visual + CSS inspection)**:
- **Color-blocked testimonials**: Each testimonial sits inside a bold solid-color panel — lavender-gray (`#F5F5FA`), peach-apricot (`#FFBB7E`), cobalt blue (`#3E6AEF`), and navy (`#00122E`) — rotating down the page. Quote text stays in a classic old-style serif with dark text on light panels and white text on dark panels. This is Retell's most distinctive signature.
- **Navy "Our Solution" comparison card**: In a three-card comparison, two competitor options sit in light lavender-gray while Cartra's approach sits in deep midnight navy with white text and peach-orange checkmarks. The dark panel visually claims authority.
- **Cool cobalt + warm peach**: The primary accent is electric cobalt blue (`#3E6AEF`) on white backgrounds (buttons, eyebrow tags, links). Warm peach (`#FFBB7E`) serves as the counterpoint for entire section backgrounds. The cool/warm tension is the brand signature.
- **Eyebrow tags in cobalt**: Small labels above section headings ("Overview", "Highlights") appear in cobalt blue, all-lowercase, creating rhythm between sections.
- **Condensed display serif option**: Hero uses "Denton Condensed" at 108px for dramatic editorial scale. Mixed with "Untitled Sans" for supporting headlines and body. Cartra is going heavy grotesque for display, not condensed serif — but the *pairing* of a sans for body + serif for long-form editorial content (testimonials, quotes) carries over.
- **Mesh/aurora gradients behind product screenshots**: Soft multi-hue gradient backgrounds (purple/blue/peach blurs) sit behind product UI mockups inside feature cards. Distinctive way to frame screenshots without hard borders.
- **Editorial list layout**: Feature sections with "Lowest Latency", "Ultra Realistic Voice" as list rows — eyebrow label → description → tiny graphic accent on the right. Clean, confident, scannable.
- **Serif testimonials with photos**: Each testimonial pairs a large serif quote on the left with a real photo of the person on the right. Name + company logo + avatar sit at the bottom. Restrained and human.

**Overlap between Ramp and Cursor (definitely adopt for Cartra)**:
- Warm off-white backgrounds, near-black warm text
- Multi-tint card backgrounds in bento sections (not uniform white)
- Confident display type with generous sizing
- Real product/process UI as the hero visual content
- One distinctive accent color reserved for specific uses
- Tinted-gray surrounding surfaces rather than hard borders

**Where Ramp and Cursor diverge (Cartra picks a lane)**:
- **Display type weight**: Ramp goes heavy/bold; Cursor goes light (400). Cartra should pick based on tone — bold = declarative authority, light = refined confidence.
- **Body type**: Ramp uses sans; Cursor uses serif. Serif would make Cartra stand out more in the AI-services pack but is a bolder commitment.
- **Accent color usage**: Ramp uses chartreuse only on primary CTAs; Cursor uses orange more freely on links. Cartra should default to Ramp's stricter discipline.

**Anti-references** (what NOT to look like):
- Generic SaaS landing pages with gradient hero sections and floating dashboard mockups
- "AI company" sites with cyan-on-dark, particle effects, and neural network graphics
- Cookie-cutter templates with icon grids and identical feature cards
- Uniform all-white card grids with hard borders
- Overdesigned sites where animation distracts from substance
- Plain logo clouds with naked monochrome logos floating on a page
- Testimonials styled as ornate pull quotes with giant quotation marks

### Design Principles

1. **Sharp, not flashy.** Every design choice should feel deliberate and earned. No decoration for its own sake. If an element doesn't serve comprehension or credibility, remove it.

2. **Data over promises.** Show results, not abstractions. Concrete numbers, real outcomes, specific processes. The design should frame data as the hero — not stock illustrations or abstract graphics.

3. **Confident restraint.** A tight palette with one bold accent. Generous whitespace. Large typography. The confidence comes from what you *don't* add. Two strong colors beat five safe ones.

4. **Motion with purpose.** Animation should reveal information, demonstrate process, or reward interaction — never just "look cool." Staggered reveals on scroll, animated metrics, and subtle hover states. Every motion should answer: "what does this help the user understand?"

5. **Hierarchy is king.** The page should be scannable in 10 seconds. Clear visual hierarchy through type scale, weight contrast, and spatial rhythm. Decision-makers skim — make the important things impossible to miss.

### Color Direction

Committed palette — adopted from Retell AI's color system (cool-neutral base with electric cobalt primary and warm peach counterpoint). Use these as starting values, refine during implementation:

| Token | Value | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `oklch(99% 0.003 260)` ≈ `#FCFCFE` | Primary background — very subtle cool-warm neutral off-white |
| `--surface` | `oklch(96% 0.007 270)` ≈ `#F5F5FA` | Secondary surface — cool lavender-tinted light gray (Retell's signature alt surface) |
| `--surface-peach` | `oklch(82% 0.09 60)` ≈ `#FFBB7E` | Bold warm color-block panel (testimonials, highlight sections) |
| `--surface-navy` | `oklch(18% 0.04 255)` ≈ `#00122E` | Deep midnight navy panel (dark "our solution" cards, contrast sections) |
| `--surface-navy-2` | `oklch(23% 0.045 255)` ≈ `#12233D` | Slightly lighter navy for sub-surfaces on dark panels |
| `--text` | `oklch(22% 0.03 255)` ≈ `#1C2A44` | Primary text — navy-tinted dark, cohesive with surface-navy |
| `--text-muted` | `color-mix(in oklab, var(--text) 60%, transparent)` | Secondary clause text, body copy |
| `--text-on-dark` | `oklch(99% 0.003 260)` | White-ish text on navy panels |
| `--text-on-peach` | `oklch(22% 0.03 255)` | Navy text on peach panels (high contrast) |
| `--border` | `color-mix(in oklab, var(--text) 8%, transparent)` | Subtle separators |
| `--accent` | `oklch(54% 0.19 262)` ≈ `#3E6AEF` | **Electric cobalt** — primary CTAs, links, eyebrow tags |
| `--accent-ink` | `oklch(44% 0.17 262)` | Cobalt hover/pressed state |
| `--accent-soft` | `oklch(92% 0.02 0)` ≈ `#FFDEDE` | Very pastel pink — tertiary highlight tint |

**On the palette (Retell-inspired)**:
- **Cool-neutral white base** — replaces the warm cream from earlier direction. Retell's `#FFFFFF` + `#F5F5FA` combination reads as clean, modern, and technical without feeling cold or clinical.
- **Navy + cobalt + peach trio** — the distinctive move. Cobalt blue is the primary accent (CTAs, links), peach/apricot is the warm counterpoint used for entire color-blocked sections, and deep midnight navy provides the dramatic dark contrast. The cool/warm pairing of cobalt × peach is the most memorable visual signature.
- **Multiple accent surfaces, not a single accent**: Unlike Ramp's disciplined single-accent approach, Retell uses peach and cobalt as entire-section background colors. This is bolder and more energetic — fitting for Cartra's "ahead of the curve" positioning.
- **Navy-tinted text** instead of pure black — all text derives from the same hue family as the navy dark surfaces, keeping the palette hyper-cohesive.

**Signature color-blocking pattern** (adopted from Retell):
Use bold solid-color section panels to break up the page rhythm. Recommended application:
- Testimonials: rotate across **lavender-gray**, **peach**, **cobalt blue**, and **navy** panels — each testimonial gets its own dramatic background color. Quote typography stays in the serif for editorial quality.
- Comparison/highlight sections: use the **navy panel** for "Cartra's approach" to visually dominate over competing/alternative options in lighter panels.
- Product feature sections: can sit on the default white with cards, or inside the lavender-gray surface for softer grouping.

**Alternative accent candidates** (if pure Retell palette proves too close to the reference):
- Shift primary from cobalt to a deeper ink blue `oklch(38% 0.15 258)` for more seriousness
- Shift peach to a warmer terracotta `oklch(64% 0.14 40)` for more grounded confidence
- Keep the navy + cobalt + peach system but tune each hue to differentiate from Retell directly

### Typography Direction

Committed pairing — heavy grotesque display + editorial serif body (Ramp display weight + Cursor body choice):

- **Display**: A heavy, confident grotesque sans. Used for all headings, navigation, CTAs, UI labels.
  - **Primary recommendation**: **PP Neue Montreal** (Pangram Pangram) — geometric grotesque with subtle character, used by high-end brand projects, avoids the reflex-ban list.
  - **Free alternative**: **Geist** (Vercel, open source) — modern grotesque with personality.
  - **Premium alternative**: **Söhne** (Klim Type Foundry) — most distinctive but commercial license.
  - Weight: 500–700 for display headings. Tight tracking (-0.02em to -0.03em). Large sizes.

- **Body / editorial**: An old-style serif at 400 weight. Used for longer paragraphs, testimonial quotes, and editorial body copy.
  - **Primary recommendation**: **Source Serif 4** (Adobe, free on Google Fonts) — warm, highly legible, slight editorial character, pairs cleanly with modern grotesques.
  - **Premium alternative**: **Tiempos Text** (Klim Type Foundry) — best-in-class editorial serif if budget allows.
  - Weight: 400. Line-height 1.55–1.65 for reading comfort. Body text at 55% opacity of `--text`.

- **Hierarchy rules**:
  - Large size contrast between heading and body — aim for at least a 2.5× jump from body (17px) to display (42px+).
  - Two-part headlines: use `--text` (100%) for the key phrase and `--text-muted` (55%) for the secondary clause. Borrow Ramp's move.
  - Italic emphasis inside display headlines on occasion ("built to *extraordinarily* accelerate operations") — borrow Cursor's move. Use sparingly.
  - All-caps only for small labels (nav, eyebrow tags), never for long runs.

### Layout Direction
- **Bento-style sections**: Modular card grids with varied sizes and *varied background tints per card*. This is the most distinctive visual move to import from Ramp. No uniform white card grids.
- **Asymmetric compositions**: Left-aligned text paired with right-aligned visuals. Hero sections with text on one side, product mockup on the other.
- **Soft panels, not hard boxes**: Cards use medium rounded corners, tinted backgrounds, and no visible borders. Shadows are barely perceptible.
- **Generous spacing**: Let content breathe. Whitespace signals confidence.
- **Product/process diagrams as hero content**: Show real agent workflows, approval chains, data flows — not stock illustrations or abstract shapes.
